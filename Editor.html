Clinical Study Report · ICH E3
A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Phase III Study of Veltrazine in Treatment-Resistant Hypertension
Protocol HEL-VLZ-301 · Sponsor: Heliocare Pharma · Report version 0.7 (draft)

1Synopsis

This Phase III study evaluated the efficacy and safety of veltrazine, an oral once-daily aldosterone synthase inhibitor, in adults with treatment-resistant hypertension inadequately controlled on three or more antihypertensive agents. A total of 642 participants were randomized 1:1 to veltrazine or matching placebo for 24 weeks.

The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in mean seated automated office systolic blood pressure at Week 12. Veltrazine demonstrated a statistically significant reduction relative to placebo, with a favourable tolerability profile consistent with earlier Phase II findings.

2Introduction

Resistant hypertension affects an estimated 10–15% of treated hypertensive adults and is associated with substantially elevated cardiovascular risk. Despite the availability of multiple drug classes, a meaningful proportion of patients do not achieve guideline-recommended blood-pressure targets, motivating the development of agents with novel mechanisms of action new approaches.

Veltrazine selectively inhibits aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2), reducing aldosterone production while sparing cortisol synthesis. This report presents the full results of the pivotal HEL-VLZ-301 study conducted across 84 sites in 11 countries between March 2024 and February 2026.

3Study objectives

3.1Primary objective

To compare the effect of veltrazine versus placebo on the change from baseline in mean seated automated office systolic blood pressure (SBP) at Week 12 in adults with treatment-resistant hypertension.

3.2Secondary objectives

  • Change from baseline in mean seated office diastolic blood pressure at Week 12 and Week 24.
  • Proportion of participants achieving SBP < 130 mmHg at Week 24.
  • Change from baseline in 24-hour ambulatory systolic blood pressure at Week 12.
  • Safety and tolerability, including incidence of hyperkalaemia and changes in serum potassium.

4Investigational plan

4.1Overall study design

This was a multicentre, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study comprising a 4-week single-blind placebo run-in followed by a 24-week double-blind treatment period. Eligible participants were randomized 1:1 to veltrazine 50 mg once daily or matching placebo, stratified by baseline SBP and number of background agents.

4.2Study population

The study enrolled adults aged 18 to 80 years with a documented diagnosis of resistant hypertension, defined as seated office SBP ≥ 140 mmHg despite stable doses of at least three antihypertensive agents, one of which was a diuretic.

4.2.1Eligibility criteria

Participants were required to have an estimated glomerular filtration rate ≥ 45 mL/min/1.73m² and a serum potassium ≤ 4.8 mmol/L at randomization. Key exclusions included secondary hypertension, recent cardiovascular events, and pregnancy or breastfeeding.

5Efficacy evaluation

At Week 12, veltrazine reduced mean seated office SBP by 14.2 mmHg compared with 5.1 mmHg for placebo, a placebo-adjusted difference of 9.1 mmHg (95% CI 6.8 to 11.4; p < 0.001). Reductions were sustained through Week 24 and were consistent across prespecified subgroups, including participants aged 65 years and older.

6Safety evaluation

Veltrazine was generally well tolerated. The most frequently reported adverse event was mild hyperkalaemia, occurring in 6.4% of veltrazine-treated participants versus 1.9% on placebo; no events were serious and all resolved with dose adjustment or dietary measures. No deaths were considered related to study treatment.
